Food culture · Seasonal traditions

What is Kimjang? The seasonal tradition of making and sharing kimchi

Kimjang is the seasonal practice of preparing and sharing kimchi for the winter. UNESCO describes it as more than a recipe: it is a cultural practice through which people share work, food and knowledge.

Reviewed: 11 October 2026 · SEOUL NOTE

UNESCO inscribed “Kimjang, making and sharing kimchi in the Republic of Korea” on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2013. UNESCO describes it as a seasonal practice in which communities make large quantities of kimchi in late autumn and share it so households have food for winter.

Seasonal preparation and shared work

UNESCO’s account describes ingredients being prepared through the year: seafood for salting and fermenting in spring, sea salt in summer, and dried red peppers toward the end of summer, followed by communal kimchi-making in late autumn. This is a cultural overview, not a claim that every household follows the same calendar or method.

There is no single “Korean kimchi” flavour

UNESCO notes regional differences and family-specific methods and ingredients. It would therefore be misleading to treat all kimchi as one uniform recipe. Ingredients, seasoning, fermentation and the vegetables used can vary.

What the practice represents

UNESCO explains that Kimjang can strengthen cooperation within families and communities and connect people with seasonal resources. Heritage recognition describes the cultural significance of a practice; it does not mean every person in Korea takes part in the same way or continues the tradition today.
