# Korean emergency and visitor-help contacts — 11 Oct 2026

**Review date:** 11 October 2026 (Asia/Seoul)  
**Audience:** Japanese- and English-reading visitors. This is general contact information, not a substitute for emergency responders, a medical decision, or confirmation that a line/app is currently available.

## What the public pages say

- **112 — police emergency.** Japan’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MOFA) Korea safety data and Korea Tourism Organization (KTO/VISITKOREA) safety guidance identify 112 for police.
- **119 — fire and ambulance/emergency response.** MOFA and KTO safety guidance identify 119 for fire and ambulance. In a threat to life or property, the KTO safety guide says to call 112 or 119 directly. The homepage uses this direct-emergency distinction.
- **1330 — KTO tourism information, interpretation and travel-related complaints.** The Japanese-language VISITKOREA 1330 page describes telephone and text-chat travel assistance and lists domestic `1330` and overseas `+82-2-1330`. Government24’s service page, last updated 27 July 2026, confirms the same numbers and describes nationwide tourism information, interpretation and tourism-complaint support. The site labels 1330 as non-emergency and does not use it as a substitute for 112/119.
- **Emergency-language guidance.** KTO’s “Disaster Safety Services for International Visitors in Korea” (2 October 2025) advises visitors who do not speak Korean to say “English please” or “Interpreter please” after connecting to emergency services, and to identify their location using a nearby building or phone GPS. It describes the multilingual Baro Emergency Reporting app, including Japanese. The site does not promise a particular language, connection time or availability in every case and does not present an app as a replacement for a call.
- **Korean phrase shown on the page:** `일본어 통역을 부탁드립니다.` is offered with the editorial translation “Please connect me with a Japanese interpreter.” It is a phrase to show, not an official script or a guarantee of interpretation.

## Source checks

1. [MOFA Japan — Korea safety data, emergency-contact section](https://www.anzen.mofa.go.jp/info/pcsafetymeasure_003.html#menu_5) (Japanese; page updated 9 July 2025; directly fetched and checked 11 October 2026). The section identifies 112 as police, 119 as fire/emergency, and 1330 as tourism interpretation/travel information. It says a caller to 112 can ask for Japanese after the operator answers, via a three-way call with an interpreter; the site does not promise this option for 119 or every call. The page also contains broader safety and entry information; the site links directly to the contact section and does not copy its dated entry-rule details.
2. [VISITKOREA — 1330 tourism interpretation and complaints](https://japanese.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?menuSn=454&vcontsId=140643) (Japanese). Checked 11 October 2026. Describes 1330 by phone/text chat, travel information, interpretation and tourist complaints; lists `1330` in Korea and `+82-2-1330` from overseas, with call/chat channels.
3. [Government24 — 1330 Korea Travel Helpline](https://www.gov.kr/portal/service/serviceInfo/B55101100019) (Korean; last modified 27 July 2026). Confirms domestic and overseas numbers and the service scope.
4. [VISITKOREA — Disaster Safety Services for International Visitors in Korea](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=248145) (English; dated 2 October 2025). Confirms 112/119, interpreter request guidance, location guidance and emergency apps. Its Japanese-domain counterpart displays the same English article text, so the Japanese home page labels the source as English.
5. [VISITKOREA emergency FAQ](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/faq/faqMainView.do?menuSn=404&pstSn=94) (English). This page also discusses 1339 in a medical context but provides no visible update date; the published contact card does not reproduce that additional guidance. It uses 112/119 for emergencies and 1330 for non-life-threatening travel/interpretation help. For urgent situations, the public copy points directly to the 112/119 contacts supported by MOFA and the dated KTO safety article.

## Implementation notes

- Japanese and English home pages show tappable `tel:112` and `tel:119` emergency links, and a visually distinct non-emergency 1330 section.
- The Japanese page links to MOFA’s Japanese contact section, the Japanese VISITKOREA 1330 page, Government24, and KTO’s dated safety article. Its on-page language note reflects MOFA’s 112-specific advice to request Japanese after the police operator answers; it does not extend this to 119. The English page links to the English KTO emergency FAQ and safety article, Government24 (Korean), and the Japanese 1330 guide with the language identified in its label.
- The pages do not claim 24-hour service, free calling, guaranteed interpretation, a particular response time, or guaranteed app availability. Carrier and data charges may apply depending on the connection method.
- Recheck current telephone/contact details, language options and app availability just before publication. The Japanese MOFA contact section was directly checked on 11 October 2026.
