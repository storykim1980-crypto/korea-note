Travel · Before you go

Before entering Korea: K-ETA and the e-Arrival Card explained

K-ETA and the e-Arrival Card are separate processes. This is a reference for short-stay visitors travelling on a Japanese ordinary passport, checked on 11 October 2026. Requirements vary by nationality, passport, purpose and length of stay.

Reviewed: 11 October 2026 · SEOUL NOTE

In brief: a temporary K-ETA exemption does not waive visa or stay requirements. It also does not automatically settle whether you must submit an e-Arrival Card. Check each requirement separately.

1. Check the current K-ETA exemption

The Korea Tourism Organization’s visa and travel-requirements page lists Japan among nationalities temporarily exempt from K-ETA through 31 December 2026. This is time-limited information about applying for K-ETA. For travel after that date—or if the policy changes—check the current eligible-country list and notice on the official K-ETA site.

2. Use the official e-Arrival Card Navigator

The official Korean e-Arrival Card portal says eligible travellers can submit the free online declaration within three days before arrival. Exemptions depend on which valid entry document you hold, so select your situation in the official Navigator and check whether a declaration is required for your arrival. Do not assume that being temporarily exempt from applying for K-ETA also exempts you from the e-Arrival Card.

3. Check visa and permitted-stay rules separately

Visa requirements and permitted stay depend on nationality, passport type, purpose and individual circumstances. Check the Korea Visa Portal and the latest information from the Korean mission responsible for your country. If you are unsure, contact an official immigration or consular source.

Before departure: 1) confirm your passport, purpose and stay conditions; 2) check the current K-ETA exemption list; 3) use the official e-Arrival Card Navigator; 4) submit through the official portal if it says you need to.

The e-Arrival Card service is free. Be cautious of unofficial sites that ask for payment details; confirm that the address bar shows the official e-arrivalcard.go.kr domain.

The K-ETA exemption note above reflects the 2026 information checked on 11 October 2026. Entry requirements can change. Recheck official portals before booking, boarding and travel. Final admission is determined by Korean authorities.
